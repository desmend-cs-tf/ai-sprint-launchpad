# 12 · Sprint Apps: Scale-Up Resources

These are starting points for validation and growth, not endorsements or confirmed partnerships. Contact the listed organizations through their public program or contact pages; ask for a short discovery interview or supervised pilot, not immediate adoption. Verify the currentness, licensing, geography, and permitted use of every dataset. Do not use real people's sensitive records as test data.

## 1. OpenDoors

[OpenDoors](https://open-doors-ai.replit.app/) helps beginners identify routes into entry-level tech work. **Next test:** ask job seekers and workforce coaches whether the recommendations lead to applications, training, or interviews; measure follow-through, not only clicks.

- [O*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/) — occupational skills, tasks, and role data to map transferable skills.
- [CareerOneStop Web API](https://www.careeronestop.org/Developers/WebAPI/web-api.aspx) — occupations, training, and local career services.
- [Apprenticeship.gov](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/) — registered apprenticeship pathways and search tools.
- [Chicago Cook Workforce Partnership](https://chicookworks.org/) — potential local workforce-coach and job-seeker pilot conversations.
- [Per Scholas](https://perscholas.org/) — potential training-provider feedback on accessible entry paths.
- [Year Up United](https://www.yearup.org/) — potential learner and career-readiness feedback; request an introduction through its public channels.

## 2. BenefitsLetterDecoder

[BenefitsLetterDecoder](https://benefits-letter-decoder-tirsit-christina.replit.app/) explains government notices in plain language. **Next test:** have benefits navigators review explanations against the source notice and current agency rules. Do not promise eligibility or deadlines; distinguish explanation from legal advice and securely delete uploaded notices.

- [USDA SNAP state directory](https://www.fns.usda.gov/snap/state-directory) — official state agency contacts and links.
- [USA.gov benefits](https://www.usa.gov/benefits) — federal benefits information and official starting points.
- [Legal Services Corporation: find legal aid](https://www.lsc.gov/about-lsc/what-legal-aid/i-need-legal-help) — referral path when a notice needs legal support.
- [Code for America: safety net](https://codeforamerica.org/programs/social-safety-net/) — civic technology expertise and potential discovery conversations.
- [PlainLanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/) — practical standards for clear government communication.
- [211](https://www.211.org/) — local human-service referral network to include as a human-help option.

## 3. RxExplain

[RxExplain](https://rxplainapp.replit.app/) helps users understand a prescription coverage denial. **Next test:** ask pharmacists, benefits counselors, and patient advocates to review explanations. Explain appeal routes and uncertainty; never recommend stopping or changing medication, and do not diagnose.

- [FDA Drugs@FDA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/) — official drug approval and labeling information.
- [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/) — current FDA-submitted drug labeling.
- [HealthCare.gov: appeal an insurer decision](https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/) — official private-plan appeal overview.
- [Medicare: appeal a coverage decision](https://www.medicare.gov/claims-appeals/how-to-appeal-a-medicare-decision) — official Medicare appeal steps.
- [Patient Advocate Foundation](https://www.patientadvocate.org/) — potential patient-support and navigator feedback.
- [MedlinePlus health insurance](https://medlineplus.gov/healthinsurance.html) — accessible background material and health-literacy reference.

## 4. SecondLook

[SecondLook](https://second-look--ashleydupoux17.replit.app/) teaches children to assess real and AI-generated images, with teacher moderation. **Next test:** run a teacher-supervised lesson and evaluate learning before/after, accessibility, and whether children understand that detectors can be wrong. Minimize data collection and avoid face uploads.

- [C2PA](https://c2pa.org/) — open content provenance standards; credentials can help but their absence proves nothing.
- [NIST: reducing risks posed by synthetic content](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-4.pdf) — technical guidance on provenance, labeling, detection, and synthetic media risks.
- [CIFAKE dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hammaadn/cifake) — small research dataset for prototyping; check its card/license and do not treat it as classroom ground truth.
- [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education) — digital citizenship materials and potential educator feedback.
- [ISTE](https://iste.org/) — educator network and digital learning practice resources.
- [FTC Children's Privacy (COPPA)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy) — privacy requirements to review before collecting information from children.

## 5. Candidate Reviewer

- [FEC OpenFEC API](https://api.open.fec.gov/developers/) — campaign finance records; useful context, not a proxy for policy positions.
- [Congress.gov API](https://api.congress.gov/) — bills, sponsors, and congressional records.

## 7. SafeSpace

Keep drafts private and user-controlled, clearly route emergencies to live help, and do not present the app as monitored crisis care.

## A practical pilot path

1. Pick one audience and one narrow workflow; recruit through a trusted organization with permission, not by exporting its member list.
2. Ask five people to walk through the current prototype. Pay or otherwise fairly recognize community expertise where possible.
3. Agree on a small success measure, data-retention rule, source review, and escalation/rollback plan before a pilot.
4. Publish what the app can and cannot do, where its information comes from, when data was last updated, and how to report a problem.
5. Share results with participants and partners, including failures and changes made. Ask before using an organization’s name or logo.
